# Moose Stir-Fry

A fast way to use cooked moose (leftover roast works): vegetables and meat in a soy–chili sauce. Mild unless you add heat.

## Ingredients

- **Sauce:** 1/2 cup less-sodium soy sauce
- **Sauce:** 2 Tbsp beef broth or cold water
- **Sauce:** 1 Tbsp chili paste (e.g. sambal oelek)
- **Sauce:** 1 Tbsp rice wine vinegar
- **Sauce:** 1 Tbsp brown sugar
- **Sauce:** 2 tsp sesame oil
- **Sauce:** 1 Tbsp cornstarch
- 1 1/2 Tbsp vegetable oil, plus ~1 tsp for the wok
- 1 yellow onion, thinly sliced
- 5 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1 small bell pepper, thin strips
- 1 large celery rib, thinly sliced
- 1 cup sliced mushrooms
- 1 cup snow peas
- 3/4 lb cooked moose, bite-sized pieces (~3 cups)
- Green onion and sesame seeds, to garnish
- To serve: rice or noodles

## Steps

1. Whisk the soy, broth, chili paste, vinegar, sugar and sesame oil; whisk in the cornstarch.
2. Wipe the wok with ~1 tsp oil, then heat 1 1/2 Tbsp oil over medium-high.
3. Stir-fry the onion, garlic, pepper, celery, mushrooms and snow peas about 3 minutes, until tender-crisp.
4. Re-whisk the sauce; add it with the moose. Stir 2–3 minutes until the meat is hot and the sauce thickens.
5. Garnish and serve over rice or noodles.

## Notes

- 2026-11-14: Cooked beef roast can stand in for moose. Swap in 3–4 cups of any veg (broccoli, carrots, cabbage). No added salt; the soy covers it. Add chilies or sriracha for heat. A big skillet works instead of a wok. Prep everything first — it cooks fast.
